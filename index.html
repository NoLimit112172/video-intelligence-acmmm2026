ABOUT THE TUTORIAL

Beyond short clips.
Toward a world in motion.

Video understanding is moving beyond answering questions about short, third-person clips. Today’s models must reason over time, process continuous streams, and make sense of the world from an agent’s own perspective.

This tutorial brings these developments together through tasks, datasets, methods, and evaluation. We connect foundation VideoLLMs and reasoning-oriented post-training with long-video perception, streaming interaction, and spatial and embodied intelligence.

We close by examining what remains open: making video intelligence more trustworthy, efficient, and useful in real-world settings.

01

Reasoning

Foundation VideoLLMs, post-training, reinforcement learning, and temporal reasoning.

02

Streaming

Long videos, token compression, memory, retrieval, and continuous interaction.

03

Spatial intelligence

Egocentric perception, spatial understanding, and embodied perception–action loops.

WHO IS IT FOR?

Researchers, graduate students, and practitioners in computer vision, NLP, multimedia, and robotics. Familiarity with deep learning, transformers, and vision-language models is recommended.

TUTORIAL PROGRAM

An afternoon of video intelligence.

Preliminary program

Tuesday, 10 November 2026 · 16:00–18:00

All times are in Rio de Janeiro local time (BRT, UTC−3).

Sessions are listed in presentation order. Topic assignments are provisional; individual session timings will be announced.

OPENINGIntroduction & OverviewFrom VideoQA to video understandingYicong Li+

An introduction to the task family and its evolution, followed by the three shifts that frame this tutorial: reasoning-oriented post-training, long and streaming interaction, and spatial and embodied intelligence.

PART IFoundation VideoLLMs & Video ReasoningModels, post-training, and reinforcement learningJunbin Xiao+

Temporal encoding and long-context modeling; instruction tuning, preference optimization, and distillation; reinforcement learning for video reasoning, reward design, and test-time scaling. We also examine the limits of generalization.

PART IILong/Streaming Video & Spatial IntelligenceMemory, continuous perception, and embodied understandingWeijian Ma+

Efficient long-video perception through compression, memory, and retrieval; streaming recall and proactive responses; egocentric observations, spatial representations, navigation, and closed-loop perception–action reasoning.

PART IIIFrontiers & Future DirectionsTrustworthiness, efficiency, and open challengesYicong Li+

Grounding answers in video evidence, consistency across questions and viewpoints, efficient adaptation, and cloud–edge communication. We conclude with research opportunities for reliable real-world video assistants.
